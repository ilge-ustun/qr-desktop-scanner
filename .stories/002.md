# Story 002: Phone, SMS and geo codes get cards of their own, with a Copy per value and Open in Maps for valid coordinates

## Symptom

Read from the files at `bc6c95f`, the tip of `epic/cards`. Nothing was run.

- **Phone (`tel:`).** `classify` makes it kind `"phone"` (`src/lib/payload.ts:42-44`), and `ResultCard` sends it to
  `GenericCard` (`src/components/ResultCard.tsx:28`). The number is the
  headline (`src/components/ResultCard.tsx:40-41`). Under it, the raw
  `tel:+31201234567` line is printed (`src/components/ResultCard.tsx:67`).
  The card's one Copy copies the raw text (`src/components/ResultCard.tsx:83`),
  so pasting gives `tel:+31201234567`, not the number.
- **SMS (`sms:`, `SMSTO:`) and geo (`geo:`).** `classify` has no branch for
  them (`src/lib/payload.ts:37-68`).
  - Without whitespace they reach the link branch
    (`src/lib/payload.ts:64-66`). They get a Link card:
    - The label is "Link · check before opening"
      (`src/components/ResultCard.tsx:42`).
    - The app-scheme caution is shown (`src/lib/urlSafety.ts:57-64`).
    - There is no headline: `domain` is the URL's hostname
      (`src/lib/urlSafety.ts:63`), and these codes have none.
    - Copy copies the whole code, and there is no Open.
  - With whitespace (S2, H2 and H3 below) they get the text card
    (`src/lib/payload.ts:67`).
- No card offers Open in Maps.

## Evidence

- **Premise.** Checked by reading the code at the epic's tip. The human asked
  for reading only, so nothing was run.
  - The claim that `sms:`, `SMSTO:` and `geo:` codes without whitespace pass
    `URL.canParse` comes from the coordinator. They checked it with node for
    story 001. It was not re-run here.
  - The criteria do not depend on that claim. On the base, every named code
    goes to `GenericCard`, and its Copy has no `aria-describedby`. So each
    criterion's instrument fails on the base, whether the code gets the link
    card or the text card.
- **The pattern to follow is the contact card.** In
  `src/components/ContactCard.tsx:43-71`:
  - the name is the `.headline` (`src/components/ContactCard.tsx:47`);
  - then comes one `li.field` per value: a Silkscreen `.field-name`, the value
    in `.mono` with an id, and a Copy whose `aria-describedby` names that id
    (`src/components/ContactCard.tsx:54-69`).

  An absent value gets no row, as `WifiCard` has no password row for an open
  network (`src/components/WifiCard.tsx:56-63`).
- **Where the new branches go.**
  - The `sms:`, `SMSTO:` and `geo:` branches go in `classify` before the link
    branch at `src/lib/payload.ts:64-66`.
  - They must not require the absence of whitespace: S2, H2 and H3 contain
    spaces.
  - The existing tests stay as they are, in particular
    `src/__tests__/payload.test.ts:11-18` (links) and
    `src/__tests__/payload.test.ts:27-29` (`tel:` is kind `"phone"` with the
    number).
- **The open path needs no Rust change.**
  - `ResultCard` gets `onOpen={ipc.openLink}` (`src/App.tsx:131`).
  - `ipc.openLink` invokes `open_link` (`src/lib/ipc.ts:19`).
  - `open_link` calls `check_openable` first
    (`src-tauri/src/commands.rs:33-34`).
  - `check_openable` accepts `https` (`core/src/url_guard.rs:9`) when the URL
    has a host (`core/src/url_guard.rs:28-30`). `maps.apple.com` is a host.

  CLAUDE.md asks for two checks on a new action:
  - The card offers Open in Maps only with an `https` link that the app builds
    from two validated numbers.
  - Rust checks the scheme again before anything opens.
- **Type.** `.headline`, `.content` and `.mono` are set in Sometype Mono
  (`src/styles.css:316-338`). `button`, `.meta` and `.field-name` are set in
  Silkscreen (`src/styles.css:73-82`, `src/styles.css:289-294`,
  `src/styles.css:391-393`).
- **Decided by the human** (the original intent for these cards):
  - Phone, SMS and map codes get cards of their own.
  - Each value has its own Copy. There is no Call or Send.
  - A `geo:` code gets "Open in Maps" via
    `https://maps.apple.com/?ll=<lat>,<lon>` when its coordinates are valid.
  - Decoded text is mono. It is never Silkscreen, never a button label, a link
    or markup.
  - Parsing goes in `src/lib`, with tests in `src/__tests__`.
- **Decided by the coordinator, under the human's delegation:**
  1. `tel:` keeps kind `"phone"`. Its card copies the number only.
  2. **`sms:` (RFC 5724).**
     - The number is the text up to `?`.
     - The message is the percent-decoded `body` parameter.
     - Decoding never throws on a malformed escape (`100%`, `%E0%A4%A`).
  3. **`SMSTO:number:message`.**
     - The number is the text up to the first `:` after the prefix.
     - The message is everything after that colon, with any further colons
       kept.
  4. With no message, the card has only the number row.
  5. **`geo:` (RFC 5870).**
     - The coordinates are the text between `geo:` and the first `;` or `?`.
       They are shown and copied exactly as written.
     - **Valid** means two numbers, optionally followed by a third, the
       altitude (`geo:52.3731,4.8922,10`). Each matches an optional `-`, then
       digits, then optionally `.` and digits. There is no hex, no exponent
       and no empty part. (Altitude: coordinator's choice, see below.)
     - The latitude is in [-90, 90] and the longitude in [-180, 180],
       inclusive.
     - The Maps link carries only those two numbers. `q=`, `;u=` and the
       altitude are dropped.
  6. An invalid geo code still gets a geo card, with Copy and no Open in Maps.
  7. **Android's search form** `geo:0,0?q=<place>` gets no Open in Maps
     (`0,0` there means "no coordinates"). Its card shows the percent-decoded
     `q` text as a mono row with its own Copy. This story reads "the search
     form" as: the coordinates are written exactly `0,0`, and the part after
     `?` has a `q=` parameter.
  8. Open in Maps calls the card's existing `onOpen` with the `https` link,
     and only on a click.
- **This story's own readings.** Not decided by the human or the
  coordinator; the human can change them before promoting.
  - **Malformed escapes.** When a `body` or `q` value is not a valid
    percent-encoding, it is shown and copied exactly as written. So S4a's
    message is `100%` and S4b's is `%E0%A4%A`. Decision 2 says only that
    decoding must not throw. Replacing bad bytes with U+FFFD, or decoding
    part of the value, would give other text.
  - **G3's place.** Whether G3's card also shows `Opera House` is the
    builder's choice. The criteria accept either.
  - **A1's coordinates.** Whether A1's card also shows `0,0` is the builder's
    choice, for the same reason.
  - **Left to the builder:**
    - the new payload kinds and their `LABELS`
      (`src/lib/payload.ts:22-29`): the app's own words, in Silkscreen;
    - the row names;
    - which value is the headline;
    - whether the three cards share a component.

    A test must not depend on any of these.
  - **Prefix case.** The prefixes match case-insensitively, as the existing
    branches do (`src/lib/payload.ts:42`). Only the spellings in the named
    inputs are tested.
- **Coordinator's choices, under the human's delegation** (the writer's two
  open questions):
  1. **A code with no number or no coordinates** (`sms:`, `sms:?body=Hi`,
     `SMSTO:`, `SMSTO::Hi`, `geo:`) does not parse and falls back to the card
     it gets today, the human's rule for codes that do not parse. The number
     is what makes an SMS parse, as the network name does for Wi-Fi
     (`src/lib/payload.ts:103-104`), and non-empty coordinate text is what
     makes a geo code parse. Decision 6 applies to geo codes that have
     coordinate text which is not valid. All five pass `URL.canParse`
     (checked with node), so today, and after this story, they are kind
     `"url"`. AC4 checks E1–E3.
  2. **Altitude.** RFC 5870 allows a third number, the altitude. A geo code
     with one is valid and gets Open in Maps with `ll=` carrying only the
     first two numbers. AC3 checks G5.
- **Size.** Five criteria, which is at the limit the human set. AC5 could be
  folded into AC2 to AC4. It is kept apart because its mutations (markup, a
  value in a label, an anchor) differ from the copy and open mutations. The
  story is not split.

## Acceptance criteria

**The inputs.** The criteria range over these codes and nothing else. They
are written as TypeScript, so the strings are exact.

```ts

// SMS
const S1 = "sms:+31201234567?body=Running%20late%3A%2010%20min";
const S2 = "SMSTO:+31201234567:Running late: 10 min";
const S3 = "sms:+31201234567";
const S4a = "sms:+31201234567?body=100%";
const S4b = "sms:+31201234567?body=%E0%A4%A";
// Geo with valid coordinates: Open in Maps
const G1 = "geo:52.3731,4.8922";
const G2 = "geo:52.3731,4.8922;u=35";
const G3 = "geo:-33.8568,151.2153?q=Opera%20House";
const G4 = "geo:90,-180";
const G5 = "geo:52.3731,4.8922,10"; // with altitude
// Geo without Open in Maps

const X4 = "geo:0x10,5";

const X7 = "geo:52.3731,4.8922&q=x";
const A1 = "geo:0,0?q=Rijksmuseum%20Amsterdam";
// Markup
const H2 = "SMSTO:+31201234567:<img src=x onerror=alert(1)>";

// No number or no coordinates: falls back

const E2 = "SMSTO::Hi";
const E3 = "geo:";
```

**Terms.** These hold for every criterion.

- **A card test** renders `ResultCard` for one code, with `vi.fn` callbacks,
  through the existing helper `card` (`src/__tests__/ResultCard.test.tsx:13-28`).
  That way `classify`, the routing in `ResultCard` and the card are all
  exercised. The test calls `cleanup()` between codes. Test names start with
  `002 ACn:`, so a selector picks only this story's tests and not story
  001's `ACn:` tests.
- **The buttons** are all the `button` elements in the document.
- **A Copy button** is a button whose accessible name starts with `Copy`. The
  test collects them before the first click, since a clicked one may then
  read `Copied`.
- **A Copy button's value** is defined as follows:
  - its `aria-describedby` names exactly one id;
  - the element with that id is in the document;
  - the value is that element's `textContent`.
- **"The card copies R (and may also copy O)"** means all of these hold:
  1. Every Copy button has a value.
  2. Clicking each Copy button once calls `onCopy` exactly once per click,
     with exactly that button's value.
  3. Every string in R is the value of at least one Copy button. A headline
     Copy and a row Copy may both carry the same value.
  4. Every Copy button's value is in R or in O.

  It follows that the raw code is never copied unless it is in R, and for
  these inputs it never is.

The builder also unit-tests the parsers in `src/__tests__`, as the human
decided. Those tests are not the instruments of these criteria.

## Known weakness of the instrument

- **Fonts are not seen.** jsdom loads no stylesheet and does no layout. AC5
  proves which class holds each value. It does not prove the glyphs are
  Sometype Mono. A new CSS rule that sets `var(--pixel)` on `.mono` inside a
  new card would pass. The reviewer should read the `src/styles.css` diff,
  and a person should look.
- **Placement is not seen.** `aria-describedby` ties each Copy to the
  element whose text it copies in the DOM. It does not show that the button
  sits next to that value on screen, or that "Copied" shows on the clicked
  button only.
- **The callbacks are mocks.** `onCopy` and `onOpen` are `vi.fn`. The
  clipboard, the Rust guard and Maps never run.
  - That `check_openable` accepts a `https://maps.apple.com/?ll=…` link is
    read from `core/src/url_guard.rs:22-32`, not run. Its tests
    (`core/src/url_guard.rs:39-48`) cover `https://example.com/a?b=c`, not a
    Maps link, and `core/` is out of scope.
  - What Apple Maps does with `ll=` alone (centre only, or a pin too) was not
    observed. The link's form is the human's decision.
- **H2 and H3 already render as text on the base.** The base gives them the
  text card, so AC5's "no `img`" check passes there. AC5 is red on the base
  for another reason: no element's own text is the value. Its `img` check
  guards only against the new cards rendering markup.
- **The raw code.** Only P1 is checked for not printing its raw code
  (`tel:`). Whether the SMS and geo cards print theirs is not tested. The
  contact and Wi-Fi cards do not.
- **Parsers are proven on the named inputs only.** These cases are not
  specified:
  - a prefix in another case (`SMS:`, `smsto:`, `GEO:`);
  - several recipients (`sms:+31…,+32…`);
  - other `sms:` parameters, or `body` given twice;
  - `+` meant as a space in `body` or `q`;
  - whitespace inside the coordinates, `-0`, or leading zeros;
  - whether the link carries the numbers as written or normalised (the named
    inputs are the same either way);
  - `geo:0,0` without `q`;
  - a `;crs=` other than WGS-84, which the "first `;`" rule drops even
    though it changes what the numbers mean;

  Odd output on any of these is a gap in the spec, not a failure of these
  criteria.
- **No real code was scanned.** The inputs come from the coordinator, written
  from RFC 5724, RFC 5870 and ZXing's `SMSTO:` form. None was captured from
  a generator or a phone.
- **Base behaviour.** The Link-card outcome for `sms:` and `geo:` without
  whitespace rests on the coordinator's node check for story 001. The rest of
  the Symptom section comes from reading the code.
